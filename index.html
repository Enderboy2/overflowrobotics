About us

Built by students. Engineered like professionals.

Overflow Robotics started in 2021 with one founder and one goal: build underwater robots good enough to compete with the best in the world. Four friends joined soon after — two of them became co-founders — and the team has since grown to more than thirty engineers. Overflow has won Egypt's national MATE ROV competition three years running and, in 2026, placed 5th in the world out of more than 60 teams.

Our mission

We push the boundaries of student-led engineering: designing advanced Remotely Operated Vehicles, holding ourselves to professional standards in mechanical design, electronics and software — and inspiring the next generation of engineers.

See how we teach the next generation

How we engineer

Every robot goes through a professional workflow: trade-off matrices to choose a concept, CAD modelling, simulation for hydrodynamics and structure, then rigorous testing in the pool and open water.

Our journey

From one founder to 5th in the world.

  1. 2021

    The beginning

    Overflow is founded with a vision of competitive, student-built underwater robots. Four friends join soon after, and two of them are recognised as co-founders — together they build the first team.

  2. 2022

    2nd place nationally

    Our first major result — second in Egypt, and proof that the engineering approach works.

  3. 2023

    National champions

    First place in the Ranger class at the MATE Egypt Regional ROV Competition.

  4. 2024

    On the world stage

    Champions of Egypt again, and 18th internationally at the MATE ROV World Championship.

  5. 2025

    Highest Egyptian ranking ever

    A third straight national title and 11th in the world — at the time, the best result by an Egyptian team in MATE ROV history.

  6. 2026

    5th in the world

    5th out of more than 60 teams at the MATE ROV World Championship — beating our own record. The same year we launched Tech in Schools (TIS) to teach what we've learned.

Our ROV

Inside our ROV

Precision-engineered systems for advanced underwater missions.

T200 thrusters

5.1 kgf

Fully flooded, pressure-tolerant thrusters in corrosion-resistant polyamide — water cools the motor and equalises pressure.

Vision system

7–8 HD cameras

A 120° camera array plus an Intel depth camera for obstacle detection, object tracking and autonomous navigation.

Precision manipulator

Sensor feedback

A high-torque gripper with fine control, gentle enough for fragile objects and strong enough for industrial tasks.

Carbon-fibre body

Light & rigid

A streamlined carbon-fibre frame that protects the electronics while keeping the ROV agile and low-drag.

ESP32 bridge

Near-zero latency

Custom control electronics linking the pilot station to the ROV with minimal delay.

Departments

Three disciplines. One robot.

Mechanical design

Frames, manipulators, thruster housings and the carbon-fibre body — designed in CAD, simulated, then built and tested.

Electrical systems

Power distribution, wiring, sensors and custom electronics boards that survive pressure and salt water.

Software & AI

Real-time control, autonomous navigation and computer vision for object recognition underwater.

Education · TIS

Tech in Schools: Real robots. Real skills.

TIS is an applied STEM and robotics track for middle and high school students, designed and taught by Overflow Robotics — the student team that placed 5th in the world at the 2026 MATE ROV World Championship. Not a course: a full applied-engineering journey — inside partner schools, or from home for any student.
